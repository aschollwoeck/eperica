# Alliances & diplomacy — fight as a group

You don't have to play alone. An **alliance** binds players into a group with a shared identity, a
roster, and **diplomacy** toward other alliances. Belonging to one — or to a **confederation** of
friendly alliances — lets you see your allies' villages, watch incoming attacks against them, and
reinforce each other. Alliances are the social and political layer of the world.

## Building an Embassy

Everything starts with the **Embassy**. Its level is the gate:

- **Level 1** lets you **join** an alliance you've been invited to.
- **Level 3** lets you **found** your own alliance.

The level that counts is your **highest** Embassy across all your villages — build it like any
other building, from the village page. See [Building & upgrading](buildings.md) for where it sits.

## Founding & joining

With an Embassy at level 3, the **Alliance** page (linked from your village) offers a **Found**
form — give your alliance a name and a short tag (shown next to your villages on the map). You
become the **Founder**.

To grow, a member with the **invite** right invites other players by name. An invited player sees
the offer on their own Alliance page and can accept or decline. You can belong to **one alliance at
a time**, and an alliance has a maximum size (around 60 members).

## Roles & rights

Members hold one of three ranks:

- **Founder** — full control of the alliance (one per alliance).
- **Leader** — holds whatever rights the Founder grants: **invite**, **expel**, **diplomacy**,
  **announce**, **manage roles**. A leader can do only what they've been granted.
- **Member** — belongs to the alliance, with no management powers.

You can only expel someone you outrank, and only the Founder (or a leader with *manage roles*) can
change ranks. The Founder can transfer the founder role to another member, or disband the alliance
entirely.

## Diplomacy

A member with the **diplomacy** right sets your alliance's stance toward another:

- **War** — declared by one side and immediately mutual.
- **Confederation** — trusted allies. One side proposes it and the other must accept before it
  takes effect. Confederates share visibility and defence with you.
- **Neutral** — the default. Either side can cancel a war or confederation back to neutral.

A pair of alliances has at most one stance at a time; declaring war clears a confederation, and you
can't propose a confederation while at war.

## Seeing & defending together

Once you're in an alliance, the Alliance page shows:

- **The roster** — your fellow members, their roles, and rights.
- **Allied villages** — the villages of your members and your confederates, so you can find them on
  the map to reinforce.
- **Incoming attacks** — every attack and raid heading for an allied village, with where it lands
  and when. You only see that something is coming and its arrival time, not the attacker's troops —
  that still takes a scout or the battle itself.

> **Tip:** Shared early warning is the heart of coordinated defence — rally reinforcements to a
> threatened ally before the blow lands.

Reinforcing an ally uses the ordinary Rally Point; alliances just make allied villages easy to find
and their danger visible. Defending troops fight exactly as always — see
[Attacking & defending](combat.md).

## Chat and the forum

Alliance members can talk in real time via [Communication](communication.md) (the alliance chat
channel), and post and discuss longer topics in the [Alliance Forum](alliance-forum.md) — both
reached from the Alliance page.

## What alliances don't do

Alliance-wide combat bonuses and dedicated war leaderboards aren't part of the system — an alliance
is about coordination and visibility, not stronger troops. See
[Ranking & statistics](ranking-and-statistics.md) for the aggregate boards alliances do get.
